## Introduction

The three small depressions found in [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) are the strongest physical-evidence claim associated with the incident and the foundation of the famous “landing gear” interpretation. According to Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt’s memorandum, investigators found three impressions roughly 1.5 inches (3.8 cm) deep and 7 inches (18 cm) across at the location where an unidentified object had reportedly been seen. The marks were said to form a triangle, a detail that immediately suggested a tripod landing arrangement to many observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia Commonsthree depressions 1 1/2&quot; deep and 7&quot; in diameter were found where the object...</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that the same evidence that appears persuasive at first glance becomes much less decisive when examined closely. The measurements were limited, the geometry was never documented in a formal survey, the marks were found in an active woodland environment, and alternative explanations emerged almost immediately. Whether the three depressions genuinely indicated a landed craft depends less on their existence—which is not seriously disputed—than on how much can be inferred from their shape, spacing and context.

## What Halt's Memo Actually Measured

The key document is Halt’s memorandum of 13 January 1981. It does not describe landing gear directly. Instead, it records that “three depressions” measuring about 1.5 inches deep and 7 inches in diameter were found where the object had allegedly been observed. The memo further notes that radiation readings were later taken in and around the impressions, including the centre of the triangle they formed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia Commonsthree depressions 1 1/2&quot; deep and 7&quot; in diameter were found where the object...</span></span></span>

Two points are often overlooked.

First, the memo gives dimensions for the individual depressions but not the distances between them. Modern retellings frequently emphasise a geometric pattern suggestive of a vehicle, yet the original document does not provide the measurements needed to establish a precise triangular footprint.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia Commonsthree depressions 1 1/2&quot; deep and 7&quot; in diameter were found where the object...</span></span></span>

Second, the memo does not state that investigators determined the marks were caused by a landed craft. It records observations: depressions, a triangular arrangement and radiation measurements. The leap from “three marks” to “landing gear” came primarily through interpretation rather than direct measurement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia Commonsthree depressions 1 1/2&quot; deep and 7&quot; in diameter were found where the object...</span></span></span>

Later accounts by participants and supporters often described the marks as evidence of a craft resting on three points. Some witnesses referred to plaster casts being taken, while later narratives increasingly presented the triangle as a structured landing pattern rather than merely a set of impressions in the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/HISTORY/posts/retired-col-charles-halt-recounts-the-unexplained-lights-he-saw-in-rendlesham-fo/10156037522506184/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Retired Col. ## Why Triangle Geometry Sounded Persuasive

The appeal of the landing-gear interpretation is easy to understand. Three points define a stable platform. Aircraft, helicopters and engineering structures commonly use tripod arrangements because three supports automatically sit securely on uneven terrain.

In the Rendlesham case, several factors reinforced that interpretation:

* The marks reportedly appeared in the same area where witnesses believed an object had been on the ground.
* The depressions were similar in reported size.
* They were described as forming a triangle rather than appearing randomly scattered.
* The marks became linked to nearby [tree damage]({{ 'tree-damage/' | relative_url }}) and later radiation readings, creating the impression of a coherent [landing site]({{ 'landing-site/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia Commonsthree depressions 1 1/2&quot; deep and 7&quot; in diameter were found where the object...</span></span></span>

Psychologically, a triangle is also a highly recognisable pattern. Once witnesses or investigators begin considering a landed craft, three roughly aligned marks naturally invite comparison with landing struts. The interpretation gains strength because it links multiple observations into a single narrative: object seen, object lands, object leaves traces.

This is one reason the depressions have remained central to Rendlesham discussions. Unlike the lights in the sky, the marks were physical features that could be photographed, measured and revisited. Even critics generally accept that the impressions existed; the dispute concerns what produced them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham5.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the landing marks17 Jan 2008 — A triangle of small indentations was located between the trees near the...</span></span></span>

## Where the Landing-Gear Reading Breaks Down

The main weakness in the landing-gear interpretation is that the triangle itself was never documented with the level of precision required to distinguish an engineered footprint from an ordinary woodland pattern.

A true landing-gear hypothesis implies several testable expectations. One would expect carefully measured spacing, evidence that the marks bore substantial weight, and documentation showing that the geometry was unusually regular. The surviving record provides little of this. The frequently repeated dimensions describe the size of each depression, not the overall pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia Commonsthree depressions 1 1/2&quot; deep and 7&quot; in diameter were found where the object...</span></span></span>

Another problem is the environment. The impressions were found in a [managed forest]({{ 'managed-forest/' | relative_url }}) where animal activity, weather and forestry operations routinely disturbed the ground. Critics noted that shallow scrapes or depressions are common features on a forest floor and argued that the marks were not extraordinary in themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/rendlesham-forest" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Rendlesham Forest Incident (26 December 1980)June 13, 2026 — The next morning, three small depressions about 7 inches across...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 2026</span></span></span>

Most importantly, alternative explanations were offered by people familiar with the site. Suffolk police officers and forestry personnel reportedly identified the impressions as rabbit diggings or [rabbit scrapes]({{ 'rabbit-scrapes/' | relative_url }}) rather than evidence of a landing. Forester Vince Thurkettle, who knew the area and later became one of the best-known sceptical voices on the case, argued that the glade contained ordinary rabbit disturbances that happened to form a rough triangular arrangement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

The phrase “rough triangular arrangement” matters. Human observers are good at finding geometric patterns, particularly when they are already searching for evidence that confirms a dramatic event. The three depressions remain an authentic part of the historical record because they were documented in Halt’s memo and discussed by multiple witnesses. They are not an invented feature added years later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia Commonsthree depressions 1 1/2&quot; deep and 7&quot; in diameter were found where the object...</span></span></span>

However, the evidential question is narrower than many retellings imply. The marks demonstrate that three shallow impressions were observed at the alleged site. They do not, by themselves, demonstrate that a vehicle landed there. To support that stronger conclusion, investigators would need evidence showing that the impressions possessed a uniquely engineered geometry or characteristics inconsistent with ordinary woodland disturbance.

More than four decades later, that evidence has not emerged. Supporters continue to regard the triangular arrangement as suggestive of landing gear, while sceptics view the marks as a classic example of ambiguous physical traces acquiring significance because they were discovered after an unusual and widely discussed sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The most notable encounter involved U.S. Air Force personnel Jim Penniston, John Burroughs, and Charles Halt, who reported strange lights...</span></span></span>
