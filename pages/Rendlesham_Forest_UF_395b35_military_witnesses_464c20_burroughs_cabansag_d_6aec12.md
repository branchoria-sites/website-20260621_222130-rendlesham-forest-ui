## Introduction

One of the most revealing features of the [first night]({{ 'first-night/' | relative_url }}) of the [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) incident is not what the witnesses thought they saw, but how their judgement of distance changed as they moved through the forest. The original statements from John Burroughs and Edward Cabansag contain a pattern that appears repeatedly in night-time observation cases: a light initially interpreted as nearby gradually becomes a much more distant object once the observers gain a better vantage point. ## What Burroughs and Cabansag described

The original witness statements show notable agreement on one point: the patrol followed a flashing light that appeared closer than it really was.

Burroughs reported seeing what he described as a beacon and moving towards it through the forest. According to his statement, the patrol followed the light for a considerable distance before concluding that it originated from a lighthouse. He estimated that they pursued it for roughly two miles before recognising its true source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2Cabansag agrees with Burroughs that they walked eastwards for two miles from t...</span></span></span>

Cabansag's account follows the same pattern. He recalled seeing a glow near what appeared to be a beacon. As the patrol advanced, some lights that seemed associated with the beacon turned out to be ordinary illuminated features in the landscape, including a farmhouse. Eventually, he stated that they reached a position where they could determine that what they had been pursuing was “only a beacon light off in the distance”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2Cabansag agrees with Burroughs that they walked eastwards for two miles from t...</span></span></span>

## Why trees and movement distort distance

Night-time distance estimation depends heavily on visual reference points. In daylight, observers judge range using terrain, texture, shadows and known objects. Inside a dark forest, many of those cues disappear.

Rendlesham Forest presented especially difficult conditions. Dense tree trunks interrupted sightlines, creating brief glimpses of lights through gaps in the woodland. A flashing source seen between trees can appear to occupy different positions as an observer walks, making it seem closer, farther away or even mobile. When the observer lacks a clear horizon, judging distance becomes largely speculative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2d.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The route into the forest‍The statements by Burroughs and Cabansag agree that they went past the farmh...</span></span></span>

Movement itself can worsen the problem. As the patrol advanced through uneven terrain, every new opening in the trees changed the apparent location of the light. A distant beacon can appear embedded within nearby woodland if the observer sees only fragments of its light. The effect encourages a natural assumption that the source lies somewhere ahead in the forest rather than several miles beyond it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham UFO – what was the flashing light?The Orford Ness lighthouse was first proposed as the culprit for the flashing UFO...</span></span></span>

Researchers examining the original statements have noted that even the witnesses' own distance estimates may have been inflated by the difficult conditions. Cabansag and Burroughs believed they had travelled a substantial distance towards the light, but the terrain and darkness could easily have made the journey feel longer than it actually was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2Cabansag agrees with Burroughs that they walked eastwards for two miles from t...</span></span></span>

## How beacon language changes the evidence

The most striking aspect of the Burroughs and Cabansag accounts is their use of the word “beacon”. That language matters because it reflects how the witnesses ultimately interpreted the light after following it.

Later retellings of the Rendlesham incident often focus on claims of a structured craft within the forest. Yet the contemporaneous statements from Burroughs and Cabansag point in a different direction. Both describe a flashing light that, after investigation, appeared to be a distant beacon rather than a nearby object. Burroughs specifically identified the source as a lighthouse, while Cabansag described reaching a vantage point from which the light was clearly distant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2Cabansag agrees with Burroughs that they walked eastwards for two miles from t...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important because it demonstrates a process of correction. The witnesses did not simply observe a light and maintain a fixed interpretation. Their understanding evolved as they gathered more information. In evidential terms, that evolution is valuable because it shows the patrol recognising and adjusting for an initial error in range estimation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2d.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The route into the forest‍The statements by Burroughs and Cabansag agree that they went past the farmh...</span></span></span>

The wider Rendlesham debate includes competing interpretations of what the beacon actually was. Sceptical investigators have long argued that the flashing light corresponded to the distant Orford Ness lighthouse, whose position and flash pattern matched many aspects of the reports.
