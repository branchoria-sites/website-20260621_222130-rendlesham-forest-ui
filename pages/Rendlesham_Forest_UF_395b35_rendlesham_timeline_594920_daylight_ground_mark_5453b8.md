## Introduction

The idea that something landed in [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) did not come directly from the [first-night]({{ 'first-night/' | relative_url }}) reports of strange lights. It emerged the following morning, when military personnel returned to the area in daylight and began looking for physical traces. Three small impressions in a clearing, together with marks on nearby trees, transformed a fleeting nocturnal sighting into a story with a specific location and an alleged landing site. That shift mattered because it gave later investigations a fixed point on the ground rather than a moving light in the darkness. From that moment onward, much of the Rendlesham narrative revolved around a particular clearing and the question of whether the marks found there really indicated that a craft had touched down. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsMemorandum by Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt to the British Ministry of Def...</span></span></span>

## The Clearing and the Three Small Impressions

According to Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt's later memorandum, personnel returned to the forest the day after the initial sighting and found [three depressions]({{ 'three-marks/' | relative_url }}) where the object had reportedly been seen. Halt described them as approximately 1.5 inches deep and 7 inches in diameter. The arrangement of these impressions became important because witnesses and later investigators interpreted them as resembling a triangular landing-gear pattern. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsMemorandum by Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt to the British Ministry of Def...</span></span></span>

The impressions themselves quickly became controversial. Suffolk police officers who viewed them reportedly considered them consistent with animal activity, while forestry personnel later argued they resembled [rabbit scrapes]({{ 'rabbit-scrapes/' | relative_url }}) or other natural ground disturbances. UFO proponents, by contrast, pointed to their apparent geometric arrangement and the fact that casts were reportedly made of the marks. The disagreement over what the depressions represented remains one of the central disputes in the case. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Hangar1publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

## Tree Marks and the Daylight Search for Traces

The daylight inspection did not focus solely on the ground. Investigators also examined nearby trees for signs that something unusual had occurred. Later accounts described broken branches and abrasions on trunks facing the clearing. These observations became part of a broader effort to identify physical evidence that could support the reports from the previous night. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The most notable encounter involved U.S. Air Force personnel Jim Penniston, John Burroughs, and Charles Halt, who reported strange lights...</span></span></span>

When Halt returned to the site during the second main investigation, attention again centred on these trees. On the audio recording made during that visit, members of the party discussed abrasions facing towards the centre of the clearing and noted broken branches overhead. The implication was that the marks might have been connected to whatever had allegedly occupied the site. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The most notable encounter involved U.S. Air Force personnel Jim Penniston, John Burroughs, and Charles Halt, who reported strange lights...</span></span></span>

Alternative explanations appeared almost immediately. Forestry worker Vince Thurkettle and other sceptical commentators argued that some of the supposed damage was consistent with ordinary forestry practices, including axe marks used to identify trees for future felling. In this interpretation, the daylight search did uncover real marks, but the marks were not evidence of a landed craft. They were pre-existing features that acquired new meaning once investigators began looking for traces of an unusual event. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The most notable encounter involved U.S. Air Force personnel Jim Penniston, John Burroughs, and Charles Halt, who reported strange lights...</span></span></span>

This difference in interpretation is crucial. The marks themselves were not discovered in isolation; they were discovered after personnel already believed that something unusual might have been present in the area. The same physical features could therefore be viewed either as corroborating evidence or as ordinary woodland features interpreted through the lens of the night's reports. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1b.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article - Ian RidpathBefore the month was out a real-life UFO encounter was to...</span></span></span>

## Why a Fixed Site Changed the Later Timeline

The discovery of the clearing changed the structure of the Rendlesham story. Before daylight, the case concerned observations of lights moving through the forest. After daylight, it involved a specific location that could be revisited, measured and tested. This gave later investigators something tangible to examine and helped bridge the gap between witness testimony and physical evidence. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsMemorandum by Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt to the British Ministry of Def...</span></span></span>

The impact of that shift can be seen in the second-night investigation. Halt's team returned not to search the entire forest but to inspect the area where the impressions had been found. Radiation readings were taken within the triangular pattern of depressions, and observations of nearby tree marks were recorded.
