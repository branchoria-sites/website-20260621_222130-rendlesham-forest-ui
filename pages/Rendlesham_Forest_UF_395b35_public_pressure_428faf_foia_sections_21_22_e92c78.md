## Introduction

When the final Ministry of Defence (MoD) UFO files were released, including material connected to the [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) incident, one of the most significant disputes was not over what the files contained but over why some records were withheld, delayed or redirected elsewhere. By the late stages of the UFO file disclosure programme, Freedom of Information Act 2000 (FOIA) exemptions under Sections 21 and 22 played a central role in shaping what requesters received and when they received it. Rather than invoking national security exemptions, the MoD often relied on arguments that information was already publicly available or was scheduled for imminent publication through official [archives]({{ 'archives/' | relative_url }}). This transformed some Rendlesham-related requests from debates about UFO evidence into debates about transparency and access to government records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/36/contents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legislation.gov.uk">[Legislation.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legislation.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act 2000Part II Exempt information · 21. ## Why Sections 21 and 22 Mattered

The final release programme for MoD UFO records coincided with growing public interest in Rendlesham and other well-known cases. At the same time, the department was transferring large collections of files to The [National Archives]({{ 'archive-map/' | relative_url }}) as part of a planned publication strategy. As a result, many requests arrived while records were already moving through the declassification and archival process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/UK/defe-24-2094-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault Documentsdefe-24-2094-1.pdfProactive transfer to the National Archives of MOD files related to UFOs and... the 1980 Rend...</span></span></span>

FOIA gave the MoD two particularly useful mechanisms:

* **Section 21** – information already accessible by other means.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/36/contents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legislation.gov.uk">[legislation.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legislation.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act 2000Part II Exempt information · 21. Information accessible to applicant by other means. 22. Information inten...</span></span></span>
* **Section 22** – information held with a view to future publication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/freedom-of-information-and-environmental-information-regulations/section-21-information-accessible-to-the-applicant-by-other-means/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ico.org.uk">[ICO+2ICO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ico.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">section 21 information accessible to the applicant by other means</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Information accessible to the applicant by other means...26 Jun 2023 — The overall purpose of section 21 is to remove information whi...</span></span></span>

These exemptions were procedurally different from secrecy-based exemptions. They did not necessarily claim the information was sensitive. Instead, they focused on how and when the information would become available.

## Already Public Material Under Section 21

Section 21 allows a public authority to refuse a request when the requested information is already reasonably accessible through another route. The Information Commissioner's Office describes it as an exemption designed to prevent FOIA from duplicating existing access arrangements. It is an absolute exemption and does not require a public-interest balancing test once accessibility is established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/freedom-of-information-and-environmental-information-regulations/section-21-information-accessible-to-the-applicant-by-other-means/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ico.org.uk">[ICO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ico.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">section 21 information accessible to the applicant by other means</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Information accessible to the applicant by other means...26 Jun 2023 — The overall purpose of section 21 is to remove information whi...</span></span></span>

In the Rendlesham context, this became relevant because some of the most sought-after documents had already entered the public domain. The best-known example was Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt's memorandum, the document that brought the incident to the attention of the British authorities. Copies of the memo had circulated publicly for years and later became available through official archival channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathAppendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the MoDThis is the text of the single-page memo written by Lt Col Halt to the UK&#x27;s Ministry of D...</span></span></span>

As more UFO material was digitised and catalogued by The National Archives, requesters could increasingly be directed to records that were already available through archive holdings rather than receiving fresh disclosure packages from the MoD itself. The exemption therefore reflected a practical administrative position: the department argued that FOIA should not be used to reproduce material already obtainable elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[The National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reportsEarly letters regarding UFO sightings · Correspondence on the Rendlesham Forest incident · Documents on U...</span></span></span>

For campaigners, however, this sometimes felt unsatisfactory. A document could technically be available, yet still require archive searches, [catalogue]({{ 'catalogue/' | relative_url }}) knowledge or paid reproduction services. Critics argued that directing requesters elsewhere occasionally created the impression of openness while still making access less straightforward than a direct release.

## Future Publication Under Section 22

Section 22 was even more significant during the final UFO-file release programme. This exemption applies when an authority intends to publish information in the future and it is reasonable to withhold it until that planned publication occurs. Unlike Section 21, it requires consideration of the public interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ico.org.uk/media2/xnkbxjwg/s22-and-22a-info-intended-for-future-publication-v-1-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ico.org.uk">[ICO+2GOV.UK Assets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ico.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">blished in the future. • Information is exempt if, at the time when the public...</span></span></span>

The MoD's UFO archive project created exactly the circumstances for which Section 22 was designed. Throughout the late 2000s and early 2010s, the department and The National Archives were carrying out staged transfers of UFO files. Files were reviewed, redacted where necessary and then released in organised batches. Public announcements repeatedly emphasised that large collections were already scheduled for transfer and publication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/podcast-transcript.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">covering UFO policy, Press stories, Parliamentary questions, sighting reports and Freedom of Information requests...</span></span></span>

A later MoD response concerning the Rendlesham incident illustrates the point. In 2015 the department stated that eighteen remaining UFO-related files were being prepared for transfer to The National Archives and were expected to be released through that route rather than through ad hoc disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f599240f0b6230268ef6d/20150511-FOI2015-03810-Rendlesham-Redacted-Final-Response.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK Assets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">20150511 FOI2015 03810 Rendlesham Redacted Final Response</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TNA), which are due to be transferred to TNA late 2015 or...</span></span></span>

From the department's perspective, Section 22 protected an orderly publication process. Officials could argue that releasing selected records early to individual requesters would disrupt a programme intended to make the material available to everyone simultaneously. The exemption therefore supported a policy of bulk archival release rather than piecemeal disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ico.org.uk/media2/xnkbxjwg/s22-and-22a-info-intended-for-future-publication-v-1-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ico.org.uk">[ICO+2GOV.UK Assets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ico.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">blished in the future. • Information is exempt if, at the time when the public...</span></span></span>

## Why These Exemptions Became Controversial

The controversy was not primarily legal. It was political and cultural.

Rendlesham had developed a reputation as Britain's most famous UFO case, and many requesters believed undisclosed records might contain decisive evidence.
