## Introduction

The question of whether anything physically landed in [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) rests largely on a surprisingly small piece of evidence: three shallow depressions found in a clearing where security personnel had earlier reported seeing an unusual object. Those marks became the centrepiece of the case’s physical-trace narrative because they appeared to form a triangular pattern and were later examined by Lt Col Charles Halt’s investigation team. Yet they also became one of the most disputed elements of the entire incident. Supporters have treated them as possible landing-gear impressions, while sceptics have argued that the marks were too small, too ambiguous and too easily explained by ordinary features of a forest floor. The debate over the depressions is therefore less about what witnesses reported seeing in the sky and more about whether the case produced convincing physical evidence on the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath+2The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathAppendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the MoDThe next day, three depressions 1.5 inches deep and 7 inches in diameter were found where...</span></span></span>

## The Three Depressions Described in the Memo

The most frequently cited description comes from Halt’s memorandum to the Ministry of Defence, written shortly after the events. According to the memo, investigators found “[three depressions]({{ 'three-marks/' | relative_url }})” approximately 1.5 inches deep and 7 inches in diameter at the location where the object had allegedly been observed. The memo further stated that radiation measurements taken later showed slightly elevated readings in the depressions and near the centre of the triangle formed by them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath+2The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathAppendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the MoDThe next day, three depressions 1.5 inches deep and 7 inches in diameter were found where...</span></span></span>

## Halt's Reaction on the Tape

One of the most revealing aspects of the landing-site question comes not from the memo but from the contemporaneous audio recording made by Halt during the forest investigation. The tape captures investigators moving through the clearing, discussing measurements and examining the alleged landing area in real time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the Halt tapeThis is a transcript of the cassette tape recording made by Lt Col Charles Halt on the ni...</span></span></span>

Rather than sounding as though he had arrived at an obvious crash or landing site, Halt appears to be evaluating something much more modest. Researchers who have analysed the recording note that the impressions themselves were small and required inspection rather than immediate recognition as extraordinary traces. The tone of the exchange suggests investigation and uncertainty rather than confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the Halt tapeThis is a transcript of the cassette tape recording made by Lt Col Charles Halt on the ni...</span></span></span>

This is significant because the tape was recorded in the moment, before decades of retelling shaped the story. It shows the party attempting to determine what the marks meant rather than treating them as self-evident proof of a landed craft. ## Why Small Marks Are Hard to Interpret

The greatest challenge for the landing-site theory is the modest scale of the impressions themselves. Marks roughly seven inches across and less than two inches deep are not inherently unusual in woodland terrain. Forest floors accumulate countless disturbances from animals, roots, weather, forestry work and human activity. Demonstrating that a particular depression was created by an extraordinary event requires more than showing that it exists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham5.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the landing marks17 Jan 2008 — A triangle of small indentations was located between the trees near the...</span></span></span>

Investigators who have revisited the case have repeatedly highlighted this problem. Astronomy writer and long-time Rendlesham researcher Ian [Ridpath]({{ 'ridpath/' | relative_url }}) argued that the supposed landing site consisted of small indentations whose origin could not be determined from their appearance alone. His reconstruction of the location emphasised how limited the physical evidence actually was compared with later claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham5.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath+2Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the landing marks17 Jan 2008 — A triangle of small indentations was located between the trees near the...</span></span></span>

The issue is not merely that alternative explanations exist. It is that the marks lacked distinctive characteristics that would clearly identify a vehicle, machine or unknown craft as the cause. There were no documented casts, no unique mechanical patterns, no associated debris and no independently verified evidence tying the impressions to a specific object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham5.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the landing marks17 Jan 2008 — A triangle of small indentations was located between the trees near the...</span></span></span>

## The Rabbit-Digging Explanation

One of the most frequently cited sceptical explanations emerged from people familiar with the local terrain. Accounts from later investigations state that the depressions were identified by some observers as rabbit diggings or similar natural disturbances. The depressions remain one of the most tangible pieces of evidence connected to the second-night investigation, but their evidential value is narrower than often assumed.
