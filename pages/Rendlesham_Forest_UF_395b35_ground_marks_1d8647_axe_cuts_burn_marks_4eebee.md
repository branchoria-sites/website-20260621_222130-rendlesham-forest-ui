## Introduction

One of the most debated pieces of physical evidence in the [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) UFO incident was the presence of marks on trees near the alleged landing area. Witnesses described what appeared to be burn marks or scorched patches on pine trunks, and these features were later cited as possible signs that an unknown object had descended into the forest. Yet almost as soon as the marks became part of the story, foresters and sceptical investigators offered a far more ordinary explanation: the trees had been marked with axe blazes as part of routine forestry management. The dispute matters because it illustrates how ordinary woodland features can acquire extraordinary significance when viewed through the lens of an unusual event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article‍The &#x27;burn marks&#x27; on the trees were axe cuts in the bark, made by the fo...</span></span></span>

## What Witnesses Thought the Tree Marks Showed

In accounts of the incident, the tree marks were often presented as supporting evidence that something physical had occupied the clearing. Alongside reports of ground depressions and broken branches, the apparent damage on nearby trunks seemed to suggest heat, force, or contact from an object moving through the trees. Later retellings frequently described them as burn marks or scorch marks, reinforcing the impression that the forest contained traces of an unusual event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/HISTORY/posts/retired-col-charles-halt-recounts-the-unexplained-lights-he-saw-in-rendlesham-fo/10156037522506184/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Retired Col. The attraction of this interpretation is easy to understand. Burn damage appears more difficult to dismiss than lights seen at night. If the bark had genuinely been scorched by an intense heat source, it would represent a physical effect requiring explanation. In the wider Rendlesham narrative, the tree marks therefore became part of a package of alleged trace evidence linking the sighting reports to a specific location in the forest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The most notable encounter involved U.S. Air Force personnel Jim Penniston, John Burroughs, and Charles Halt, who reported strange lights...</span></span></span>

## How Ranger Axe Blazes Were Used

The principal alternative explanation came from local forestry practice. Trees in [managed forests]({{ 'managed-forest/' | relative_url }}) are frequently marked before thinning or felling operations. One traditional method is an axe blaze: a deliberate cut through the outer bark that exposes lighter wood beneath. These marks help forestry workers identify trees scheduled for removal or management.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article‍The &#x27;burn marks&#x27; on the trees were axe cuts in the bark, made by the fo...</span></span></span>

When science writer and investigator Ian [Ridpath]({{ 'ridpath/' | relative_url }}) examined the Rendlesham site in the early 1980s, he reported finding numerous examples of such forestry marks in the area. According to his account, the supposed burn marks matched routine axe cuts used by foresters. He further noted that local forestry personnel identified the marks as standard management blazes rather than evidence of heat damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article‍The &#x27;burn marks&#x27; on the trees were axe cuts in the bark, made by the fo...</span></span></span>

This explanation gained additional weight because Rendlesham Forest was, and remains, a managed plantation rather than untouched woodland. Forestry operations regularly involved marking trees for future work. In that setting, discovering bark cuts on multiple trunks would not be unusual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

The sceptical argument does not require the marks to have been created after the sighting. Instead, it suggests that pre-existing forestry blazes were interpreted as significant once investigators returned to the area searching for physical traces of a reported landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article‍The &#x27;burn marks&#x27; on the trees were axe cuts in the bark, made by the fo...</span></span></span>

## Why Resin and Bark Damage Can Mislead

The forestry explanation becomes more persuasive when the appearance of pine trees is considered. Fresh axe cuts expose resin-rich tissue beneath the bark. Over time, resin can ooze from the wound, harden, darken, and create irregular textures that resemble charring from a distance. Weathering can further darken the exposed area and alter its appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article‍The &#x27;burn marks&#x27; on the trees were axe cuts in the bark, made by the fo...</span></span></span>

Ridpath specifically argued that resin bubbling into forestry cuts gave them the appearance of burn marks. Photographs he took at the site were intended to show how ordinary management blazes could be mistaken for evidence of scorching. To someone already expecting signs of a landed craft, these darkened resin-filled cuts could easily appear more mysterious than they actually were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article‍The &#x27;burn marks&#x27; on the trees were axe cuts in the bark, made by the fo...</span></span></span>

The issue is a classic example of context influencing interpretation. Viewed without prior expectations, a forester might see a routine blaze. Viewed after reports of strange lights, an observer might see damage consistent with an extraordinary event.
