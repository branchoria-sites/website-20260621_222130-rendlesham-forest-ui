## Introduction

The Orfordness lighthouse remains one of the strongest conventional explanations for part of the [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) incident. Its position, brightness and distinctive flash cycle match several reports of a distant, recurring white light seen through the trees. However, the case becomes more complicated when witness accounts move beyond a single flashing white beacon and describe red, blue or multicoloured lights, especially when those lights were reported as nearby, moving independently, or associated with a structured object. The key question is not whether the lighthouse explains anything—it clearly may explain some observations—but whether it explains all of them. The coloured-light reports mark the point where a simple one-cause explanation becomes more difficult to sustain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

## White flashes versus coloured-light reports

The lighthouse hypothesis works best when applied to a specific category of observations: a bright, intermittent light seen in the direction of the Suffolk coast. Contemporary witness statements and later analyses repeatedly note that some observers eventually recognised the distant light as a lighthouse or described it as a beacon-like source. The five-second flash pattern associated with Orfordness is one reason sceptical investigators consider it a powerful explanation for those particular sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

The difficulty arises because not all Rendlesham descriptions fit that pattern. Various accounts refer to [red lights]({{ 'red-light/' | relative_url }}), blue lights, coloured glows, or changing colours. Some versions describe a red light apparently moving through the forest. Others describe a luminous object with multiple coloured lights rather than a single white flash. These reports may be accurate, mistaken, embellished, or influenced by later retellings, but they are qualitatively different from seeing a lighthouse beam through gaps in trees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The most notable encounter involved U.S. Air Force personnel Jim Penniston, John Burroughs, and Charles Halt, who reported strange lights...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because a lighthouse explanation is strongest when its observable characteristics match the reported characteristics. A distant lighthouse naturally produces periodic flashes. It does not naturally produce the appearance of a nearby object carrying separate red and blue [light sources]({{ 'light-sources/' | relative_url }}) within the forest itself. Once reports shift from “a flashing light in the distance” to “coloured lights close by”, additional explanations become necessary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham UFO – what was the flashing light?&#x27; It&#x27;s worth recalling that the local police who were called out also reported th...</span></span></span>

## Why close-range claims need separate tests

One common mistake in discussions of Rendlesham is treating the entire event as though it involved only one observation. In reality, the incident consists of multiple reports made by different people over different periods.

A lighthouse explanation can be tested against several measurable features:

* Flash interval.

The lighthouse was visible from certain locations and flashed at a rate that corresponds with observations recorded during the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/lighthouse_visibility.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath+2Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – visibility of Orfordness lighthouseThe Orfordness lighthouse was visible from the forest edge only thr...</span></span></span>

Coloured-light reports require different tests because they involve different claims. Questions become:

* Were coloured navigation lights from aircraft visible?<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham UFO – what was the flashing light?&#x27; It&#x27;s worth recalling that the local police who were called out also reported th...</span></span></span>

These questions do not automatically validate extraordinary interpretations. They simply show that a distant lighthouse and a nearby red light are not the same observational problem. A good explanation for one does not automatically explain the other.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

## The problem of memory and evolving accounts

The coloured-light issue is complicated further by the evolution of witness narratives over time.

Many Rendlesham discussions rely on accounts given years or decades after the event. Historians of the case and both sceptical and pro-UFO commentators have noted that some later descriptions contain details that are either absent from or less prominent in the earliest available statements. This is especially relevant when assessing reports of structured craft displaying coloured lighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The most notable encounter involved U.S. Air Force personnel Jim Penniston, John Burroughs, and Charles Halt, who reported strange lights...</span></span></span>

That observation cuts in two directions.

For sceptics, it weakens confidence that every coloured-light detail reflects what was originally observed. [Memory]({{ 'memory/' | relative_url }}) can change, especially after years of discussion, media coverage and repeated retelling.

For critics of a pure lighthouse explanation, however, the same point means that the strongest lighthouse evidence usually addresses the earliest reports of flashing lights rather than every later claim that entered the story. A successful explanation of the original beacon-like observations does not automatically resolve later accounts of coloured lights and close encounters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath+2Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2Here are the original statements provided by five of the participants on the f...</span></span></span>

## How not to overstate a good explanation

The strongest sceptical analyses of Rendlesham generally do not claim that every reported light was the lighthouse. Instead, they argue that the most important and best-documented flashing-light observations can be explained through a combination of the Orfordness lighthouse, astronomical objects, environmental conditions and human perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

That approach avoids a common overstatement. If witnesses reported red, blue or multicoloured lights, the existence of those reports does not automatically prove an exotic explanation. But neither does the existence of a convincing lighthouse match mean that every coloured-light claim disappears.

The coloured-light reports therefore occupy an important middle ground in the Rendlesham debate. They do not overthrow the lighthouse explanation for the recurring white flashes. Instead, they highlight its limits.
