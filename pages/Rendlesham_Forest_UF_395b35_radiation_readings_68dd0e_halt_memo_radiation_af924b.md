## Introduction

The radiation figures in Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt’s January 1981 memorandum are among the most frequently cited pieces of physical evidence in the [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) UFO incident. Yet the numbers themselves are often presented without context. The memo reported beta/gamma readings of 0.1 milliroentgens in and around three ground depressions and 0.05–0.07 on a nearby tree. Whether those values represent unusual radiation depends on the instrument used, the measurement scale, and the local [background]({{ 'background/' | relative_url }}) level. When those factors are examined, the readings appear far less dramatic than many later retellings suggest. The central question is not whether numbers were recorded—the memo clearly says they were—but whether the recorded values indicate anything genuinely anomalous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpgEnglish: Memorandum by Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt to the British Ministry of Defense regard...</span></span></span>

## Were Halt's Radiation Numbers Actually High?

### What the memo reported

Halt’s memorandum states that investigators returned to the alleged [landing site]({{ 'landing-site/' | relative_url }}) and checked the area for radiation. According to the document, “beta/gamma readings of 0.1 milliroentgens” were recorded, with peak values in the [three depressions]({{ 'three-marks/' | relative_url }}) and near the centre of the triangle they formed. A nearby tree reportedly showed readings of 0.05–0.07 on the side facing the depressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons+2Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpgEnglish: Memorandum by Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt to the British Ministry of Defense regard...</span></span></span>

These figures became important because the memo is the principal official document associated with the case. The British Ministry of Defence retained the memo but there is no record of a subsequent official assessment of the radiation measurements themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/Lords/1997-10-14/debates/96394ce5-f0db-4448-af50-43a69fbf3c57/LieutenantColonelCharlesHaltMemorandum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum - Hansard14 Oct 1997 — The memorandum, which reported observations of unusual lights i...</span></span></span>

A key point often overlooked is that the memo presents only a handful of numbers. It does not include a full survey, a detailed methodology, calibration information, uncertainty estimates, or systematic control measurements from comparable nearby locations. As a result, the figures can be reported accurately while still being difficult to interpret scientifically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathAppendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the MoDThis is the text of the single-page memo written by Lt Col Halt to the UK&#x27;s Ministry of D...</span></span></span>

## Why Units and Rates Matter

The meaning of the radiation claim changes significantly depending on how the numbers are understood.

Many later discussions quote the memo’s “0.1 milliroentgen” figure without explaining whether it refers to an accumulated reading or a rate. Contemporary reconstructions based on the recorded investigation and the military radiation meter involved generally discuss values in milliroentgens per hour (mR/h). During the field investigation, readings around 0.03–0.04 mR/h were reportedly observed in ordinary areas, while peaks around 0.07 mR/h appeared at locations associated with the alleged landing site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Apple Podcasts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because radiation is never zero. Natural background radiation varies from place to place due to soil composition, rocks, cosmic rays, moisture, and other environmental factors. A reading can be higher than a nearby spot without being hazardous or extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1b.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In short, there was no unusual radiation at the site.Read more...</span></span></span>

One reason the numbers sound more impressive in isolation is that a value such as 0.1 can be described as “ten times background” if background is assumed to be around 0.01. Indeed, some commentators have noted that 0.1 is roughly ten times a nominal background figure. However, the actual readings reported during the field investigation appear to have been closer to 0.03–0.04 in surrounding areas and approximately 0.07 at the alleged hotspot. Under that comparison, the difference is much smaller—roughly a factor of two rather than a factor of ten.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham4a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The level of radiation of 0.1 is completely</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - the radiation readings 20.01 would be the general level of background radiation, so the 0.1 reading is...</span></span></span>

The practical implication is that the significance of the measurement depends heavily on which baseline is used.

## The Instrument Problem

The radiation measurements were reportedly taken with an AN/PDR-27, a standard military radiation survey meter. This instrument was designed primarily for detecting and monitoring radioactive contamination, not for conducting precise environmental science studies at very low background levels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

At the low end of its range, small fluctuations can be difficult to interpret. Environmental radiation naturally varies over short distances, and readings can fluctuate moment to moment. Without a carefully controlled survey, it is challenging to determine whether a modest increase represents a meaningful anomaly or ordinary variation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapedia.ai/wiki/rendlesham-forest-1980-britains-best-known-uap-case/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapedia.ai">[UAPedia - Unlocking New Realities]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapedia.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unlocking New RealitiesRendlesham Forest (1980): Britain&#x27;s Best Known UAP CaseRendlesham is one of the few UAP cases where radi...</span></span></span>

This does not mean the readings were fabricated. Rather, it means that the numbers alone cannot establish that an unusual source of radiation was present. The instrument could detect radiation, but the available record does not demonstrate that the observed values exceeded what might reasonably occur in a natural outdoor setting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1b.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In short, there was no unusual radiation at the site.Read more...</span></span></span>

## How Later Retellings Changed the Claim

Over time, the radiation figures became detached from their original context.

In many popular accounts, the claim evolved into a statement that radiation at the site was “eight to ten times background.” The phrase is memorable and sounds dramatic, but it shifts attention away from the actual recorded values and toward a relative comparison whose significance depends entirely on the chosen baseline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham4a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The level of radiation of 0.1 is completely</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - the radiation readings 20.01 would be the general level of background radiation, so the 0.1 reading is...</span></span></span>
