## Introduction

The ground marks at [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) are important because they turn the incident from a story about strange lights into a claim of a physical event. In Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt’s memo, the central trace evidence was a set of three small depressions, later checked for radiation, with marks on nearby trees also treated by witnesses as possible effects of a landing. The problem is that the same evidence is also the most vulnerable part of the case: police and forestry witnesses described the impressions as ordinary animal disturbance, and later sceptical analysis argued that the “burn marks” were forestry [axe cuts]({{ 'axe-cuts/' | relative_url }}) rather than heat damage. The fairest reading is that the marks were real enough to be noticed and reported, but not well preserved, independently tested, or documented in a way that can now carry the weight often placed on them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[The National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## What Witnesses Said They Found

The most cited description comes from Halt’s “[Unexplained]({{ 'unexplained/' | relative_url }}) Lights” memo, dated 13 January 1981. It says that after USAF security personnel reported a strange object in the forest, “[three depressions]({{ 'three-marks/' | relative_url }})” were found the next day where the object had allegedly been seen on the ground. The memo gives the measurements as about 1.5 inches deep and 7 inches in diameter, and says beta/gamma radiation readings of 0.1 milliroentgens were recorded, with peak readings in the depressions and near the centre of the triangle they formed. It also mentions a nearby tree with readings on the side facing the depressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1986/10/22165321/p79.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

That wording has shaped the case ever since. A triangle of impressions sounds like landing gear, especially when paired with a military officer’s memo, a night-time search, and later references to radiation. The National Archives describes the Rendlesham file as involving USAF personnel who claimed a UFO had landed in the forest, leaving traces including ground markings and radiation; it also notes that Halt’s memo is the key summary of those events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/ufo-transcript-aug-09.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives</span></span></span>

The marks were not the only alleged physical clues. The Halt tape, as summarised in later reporting, includes discussion of abrasions or damage on trees around the supposed landing area, with witnesses interpreting their direction as possibly facing towards the centre of the site. Halt’s group also described broken branches above ground level. In the UFO reading of the case, these details make the site look less like a random patch of woodland and more like a disturbed zone around a landed object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

The evidential value depends heavily on timing. The impressions were reportedly seen in daylight after the first night’s incident, while Halt’s radiation survey occurred later. That gap matters because forest floors are messy environments: pine needles, animal scrapes, forestry work, weather, foot traffic and curiosity can all alter a scene before anyone treats it like evidence. The National Archives also stresses a broader limitation: a single-sheet report is the only record of the event itself held there, while much of the surviving file consists of later correspondence and enquiries rather than a full forensic investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[The National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Why the Marks Look Persuasive at First

Physical traces are persuasive because they appear to outlast memory. Lights can be mis-seen, distances can be misjudged, and witnesses can disagree, but a mark in the ground feels like something that can be measured. Rendlesham’s three impressions therefore became a bridge between the sighting narrative and a harder claim: something heavy may have touched down.

Several features made that interpretation appealing:

* **A simple geometry.** Three marks can easily be imagined as tripod landing gear, especially if witnesses are already looking for evidence of a craft.
* **A senior officer’s memo.** Halt’s official status gave the description a seriousness that a civilian rumour would not have had.
* **Associated radiation readings.** Even low readings can sound dramatic when presented in a UFO context, especially to readers unfamiliar with normal [background]({{ 'background/' | relative_url }}) radiation.
* **Tree damage nearby.** Broken branches and bark marks offered a surrounding scene that seemed to support the idea of impact, heat or blast.

Yet each of these points is also open to a less exotic interpretation. Three marks do not necessarily make a precise engineered triangle; low radiation readings need a baseline and instrument context; and woodland damage is common in managed plantations. That is why the marks remain interesting but also risky evidence: they appear concrete, while the chain of interpretation around them is fragile.

## Animal and Forest Disturbance Explanations

The strongest mundane explanation is that the depressions were animal scrapes, especially rabbit diggings, later overinterpreted after the light sightings. Sceptical investigator Ian Ridpath reported that local forester Vince Thurkettle saw the marks and described them as irregular, not a symmetrical triangle, several months old, and covered with pine needles. In the same account, the site was surrounded by tall pines planted close together, which Ridpath argued made it an implausible landing place for a sizeable craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1986/10/22165321/p79.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

Police evidence points in the same direction. Ridpath’s account says the local police officers who visited the scene saw no UFO, reported the Orford Ness lighthouse as the visible light source, and attributed the marks to animals. That does not prove the witnesses saw nothing unusual earlier, but it does undercut the idea that the ground marks were obviously artificial or extraordinary when inspected by people outside the USAF group.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1986/10/22165321/p79.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

Thurkettle later gave a similar explanation in public accounts. He described the clearing as a normal glade with three rabbit scrapes that happened to be roughly triangular, and said the broken branches were not unusual in a forest. He also disputed the “burn mark” interpretation, saying the marks on trees were made by a ranger’s axe to indicate trees due to be felled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.co.uk/articles/what-happened-at-the-rendelsham-forest-incident-britain-s-answer-to-roswell" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.co.uk">[Sky HISTORY TV channel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on history.co.uk.</span></span></span>

This is not a trivial point. In a managed conifer forest, bark cuts, resin, broken branches, old animal disturbance and forestry marking practices can create a convincing-looking scene for people primed by a dramatic night-time report. Resin around an axe cut can look scorched. Animal scrapes partly covered by needles can look older or stranger than they are. Once sticks are placed beside marks and a party returns at night with instruments, the site can begin to behave socially like a “landing site” even if the marks themselves are ordinary.

## Radiation Readings Do Not Rescue the Landing Claim

The radiation readings are often presented as the feature that makes the ground marks harder to dismiss. Halt’s memo reported 0.1 milliroentgens, with peaks in the depressions and near the centre of the supposed triangle. That sounds precise, and precision can make a claim feel scientific. But a number alone is not enough. It needs comparison with background levels, clarity about the instrument, repeat readings, controls and independent verification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1986/10/22165321/p79.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

Ridpath’s later analysis argued that readings like those in Halt’s memo were consistent with natural background radiation from cosmic rays and the Earth itself, rather than evidence of anything unusual at the site. The Guardian’s later account of the Halt tape likewise notes that a reading of “four or five” clicks on the Geiger counter was low and consistent with normal background radiation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1986/10/22165321/p79.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

This does not mean Halt invented the readings. It means the readings, as recorded, do not do the job sometimes assigned to them in popular retellings. They show that a radiation meter was used and that small readings were heard or noted. They do not establish an anomalous radioactive residue from a landed object.

There is also a preservation problem. A proper environmental assessment would need a clear map of each reading, comparison samples away from the site, instrument calibration details, surface conditions and repeat measurements under controlled conditions. The surviving public record does not provide that level of documentation. The MoD’s later position, as summarised by the National [Archives]({{ 'archives/' | relative_url }}), was that the incident was not treated as a defence threat and that no further records or investigations of the event itself took place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[The National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Why Preservation Matters

The ground marks are a useful lesson in how physical evidence can lose value quickly. A forest floor is not a sealed laboratory. People walk over it, animals revisit it, needles fall, rain changes the surface, and forestry operations continue. By the time a site becomes famous, the original scene may already be contaminated or gone.

This is especially important at Rendlesham because the evidential chain was weak from the start. The marks were not apparently cordoned off as a crime scene, cast in plaster, mapped in detail, photographed with forensic scale markers, sampled under a documented protocol, or compared systematically with nearby animal scrapes. Later photographs and testimony can help, but they cannot fully restore the first condition of the site.

The later fate of the location also matters. Ridpath’s 1980s account says the landing marks had already been destroyed when trees were felled as part of normal forestry operations. Once that happened, the question shifted from “what can we test?” to “whose description of the marks is more reliable?” That is a much weaker position for any physical-evidence claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1986/10/22165321/p79.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

The preservation issue explains why believers and sceptics talk past each other. For believers, the marks belong to a broader pattern: witnesses, lights, a memo, radiation readings and later testimony. For sceptics, the marks are a classic example of ordinary traces becoming extraordinary because of context. Both sides are reacting to the same problem: the evidence is suggestive enough to keep the story alive, but not preserved well enough to settle it.

## What the Ground Marks Can and Cannot Show

The marks can show that something on the forest floor was noticed after the first sighting and treated seriously by at least some USAF personnel. They can also show how quickly a physical detail can become central to a UFO case when it appears in an official memo. On that limited point, they are historically important.

They cannot, on the surviving evidence, prove that a craft landed in Rendlesham Forest. The animal-scrape explanation is supported by police and forestry observations; the alleged tree burns have a plausible forestry explanation; and the radiation readings appear too low and too poorly contextualised to establish an anomaly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.co.uk/articles/what-happened-at-the-rendelsham-forest-incident-britain-s-answer-to-roswell" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.co.uk">[Sky HISTORY TV channel+2The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on history.co.uk.</span></span></span>

The marks therefore sit in an uncomfortable middle ground. They are not imaginary; they were reported, discussed and later folded into official and public accounts. But they are also not strong physical proof. Their main value today is not that they establish a landing, but that they reveal the case’s evidential risk: once an ordinary woodland disturbance is interpreted through the lens of a dramatic night-time event, it can become the most memorable “hard evidence” in the story.

## The Best Reading of the Trace Evidence

The most cautious conclusion is that the [Rendlesham ground]({{ 'ground-marks-35f31b/' | relative_url }}) marks were real physical features, but weak evidence for a landed object. Halt’s memo made them famous by recording their size, triangular arrangement and radiation readings. Later accounts by police, a forester and sceptical investigators gave them a more ordinary explanation: rabbit scrapes, routine broken branches and axe marks on trees. Center for Inquiry+2Sky HISTORY TV channel<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1986/10/22165321/p79.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

That does not resolve every question about what the witnesses thought they saw in the forest. It does, however, narrow what the marks themselves can responsibly support. They are best understood as disputed trace evidence whose meaning depends less on the depressions alone than on how carefully the site was recorded. In Rendlesham, that record was too thin to turn three small impressions in a forest floor into reliable proof of a landing.

    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
