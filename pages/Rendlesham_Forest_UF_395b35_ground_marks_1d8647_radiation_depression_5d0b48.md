## Introduction

The radiation readings taken around the [three depressions]({{ 'three-marks/' | relative_url }}) in [Rendlesham Forest]({{ 'rendlesham-forest/' | relative_url }}) are often presented as one of the strongest pieces of physical evidence in the UFO case. At first glance, the numbers appear to add scientific weight to the claim that an object landed in the forest. However, the significance of those readings depends less on the fact that radiation was detected and more on how the measurements compared with normal background levels, how the instrument worked, and whether proper controls were used.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_ground_marks_1d8647_radiation_depression_5d0b48-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Meter Readings illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_ground_marks_1d8647_radiation_depression_5d0b48-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_ground_marks_1d8647_radiation_depression_5d0b48-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
When examined closely, the readings become a more ambiguous piece of evidence. They were real measurements recorded by military personnel, but the available records do not show the kind of systematic testing that would normally be required to establish that the site was unusually radioactive. The debate therefore centres not on whether radiation was measured, but on whether the measurements meant anything out of the ordinary.

## What Halt's Memo Recorded

The key document is Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt's memorandum to the British Ministry of Defence, dated 13 January 1981. According to the memo, three depressions approximately 1.5 inches deep and 7 inches across were found at the alleged [landing site]({{ 'landing-site/' | relative_url }}). During a later visit, the area was checked with a military radiation survey meter. Halt reported that beta and gamma radiation readings of 0.1 milliroentgens were recorded, with the highest values occurring inside the depressions and near the centre of the triangular pattern they formed. A nearby tree also showed readings of approximately 0.05–0.07 milliroentgens on the side facing the depressions. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathAppendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the MoDThis is the text of the single-page memo. ## Why Background Levels Change the Meaning

The crucial question is whether the recorded values were unusual.

Later examinations of the case noted that the same survey reportedly found readings of roughly 0.03–0.04 milliroentgens per hour in surrounding areas, which were treated as [background]({{ 'background/' | relative_url }}) levels. The peaks around the depressions were therefore roughly double the local background rather than hundreds or thousands of times higher. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because radiation is never truly absent. Natural background radiation comes from cosmic rays, minerals in soil and rock, and naturally occurring radioactive elements. Environmental radiation measurements therefore have meaning only when compared against carefully established baseline readings. Public health guidance on radiation monitoring emphasises that background levels vary from place to place and that unaffected comparison samples are necessary before an elevated reading can be interpreted. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doh.wa.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/Documents/Pubs//320-062_zeromeas_fs.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doh.wa.gov">[Washington Department of Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doh.wa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Samples, called background</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">shington Department of HealthWhy We Don&#x27;t Report “Zeros” When Measuring RadioactivityJanuary 4, 2012 — Since background radiation level...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 4, 2012</span></span></span>

Critics of the UFO interpretation argue that the Rendlesham values were within the range that could be expected from normal environmental variation. Astronomy writer and investigator Ian [Ridpath]({{ 'ridpath/' | relative_url }}) reported consulting the UK's National Radiological Protection Board, which indicated that readings of the type recorded in Halt's memo would be expected from natural sources and did not demonstrate unusual radioactivity at the site. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1b.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In short, there was no unusual radiation at the site.Read more...</span></span></span>

Supporters of the incident's more extraordinary interpretation point out that the highest readings appeared in the depressions themselves and on a tree facing them. From that perspective, the pattern seems more important than the absolute numbers. Yet the recorded values remain relatively small, making interpretation heavily dependent on the quality of the survey rather than on the magnitude of the readings alone. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathAppendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the MoDThis is the text of the single-page memo. ## Missing Controls and Repeat Measurements

The biggest weakness in the radiation evidence is not the numbers themselves but the absence of a rigorous measurement programme.

There is no publicly available record showing:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* A systematic grid survey of the surrounding forest.
* Multiple control locations selected before measurement.
* Repeat readings over subsequent days.
* Laboratory analysis of soil samples from the depressions.
* Calibration records demonstrating instrument performance at the time of use.

</div>

Without such controls, it becomes difficult to determine whether the measured peaks reflected an unusual event or simply ordinary environmental variability. Radiation specialists routinely rely on repeated measurements because small differences can arise from instrument sensitivity, operator technique, terrain, moisture levels, and natural changes in background radiation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doh.wa.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/Documents/Pubs//320-062_zeromeas_fs.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doh.wa.gov">[Washington Department of Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doh.wa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Samples, called background</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">shington Department of HealthWhy We Don&#x27;t Report “Zeros” When Measuring RadioactivityJanuary 4, 2012 — Since background radiation level...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 4, 2012</span></span></span>

Another issue is that reports indicate similar small radiation increases were detected elsewhere in the forest, including locations unrelated to the alleged landing site. If true, that weakens the argument that the depressions contained a uniquely significant signature. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

The timing also complicates interpretation. The readings were not taken immediately after the original sighting but during a later investigation. ## Did Radiation Make the Marks Stronger Evidence?

The radiation readings undoubtedly helped transform the depressions from simple ground marks into a more enduring controversy. Numbers recorded by military personnel sound objective, and the association between the readings and the triangular pattern gave later investigators something measurable to discuss.

Yet the readings are best understood as suggestive rather than conclusive. The documented values were modest, the background comparison remains disputed, and the investigation lacked the controls normally needed to demonstrate that a site was genuinely anomalous.
